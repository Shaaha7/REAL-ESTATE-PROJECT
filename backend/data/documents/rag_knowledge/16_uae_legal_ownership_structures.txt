UAE PROPERTY OWNERSHIP — LEGAL STRUCTURES AND OPTIONS
======================================================

INDIVIDUAL OWNERSHIP
The simplest form. Property registered in one person's name.
Documents: Passport, visa (if resident), No Objection from family if married (some banks require)
Inheritance: UAE Sharia law applies unless a will is registered (important for non-Muslims)
Recommended: Register a UAE will through DIFC Courts Will Service for non-Muslims

JOINT OWNERSHIP (TWO OR MORE INDIVIDUALS)
Two or more names on the title deed.
Ownership split: Must be specified (e.g., 50/50 or any ratio)
Consent: All owners must sign for any sale or major decision
Common for: Couples, business partners, family members
Each owner gets a copy of the title deed

CORPORATE OWNERSHIP (UAE MAINLAND COMPANY)
Property owned by a UAE LLC or other corporate entity.
Requirements: Trade licence, Memorandum of Association, shareholder documents
Benefits: Asset protection, easier transfer through share sale
Tax consideration: No property tax, but corporate governance required
Recommended for: High-net-worth investors with multiple properties

FREE ZONE COMPANY OWNERSHIP
Some free zone companies can own property in designated areas.
DIFC: Specifically allows DIFC companies to own in DIFC
ADGM (Abu Dhabi Global Market): Similar in Abu Dhabi
Offshore companies: Generally cannot own UAE property (some exceptions in free zones)

WILL REGISTRATION — CRITICAL FOR EXPATS
UAE Sharia law default applies to ALL assets in UAE if no will registered.
For non-Muslims: Register will at DIFC Courts Wills Service
- Cost: AED 2,500 – 10,000 depending on complexity
- Covers: All UAE assets including property
- Without will: UAE courts apply Sharia inheritance (may not match your wishes)
For Muslims: Sharia inheritance applies by default (fixed shares)

POWER OF ATTORNEY (POA)
Essential when buyer/seller cannot attend DLD in person.
Types: General POA (broad authority) or Specific POA (for one transaction)
Requirements:
- Must be notarized in UAE by a UAE Notary Public
- If signed abroad: Must be attested by UAE Embassy in that country + UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs
- Cost in UAE: AED 500 – 1,500 (notarization) + typing fees
- Cost if signed abroad: AED 2,000 – 5,000 total (embassy + attestation)
Important: POA must be recent (not more than 2 years old typically)

TRUST STRUCTURES
Available through DIFC Courts (Trusts Law) or common law jurisdictions
Used by UHNWI for multi-generational wealth planning
Not commonly used for individual properties under AED 20M

PROPERTY GIFTING IN UAE
Parents can gift property to children:
- Transfer fee: 0.125% (not 4%) if direct family members
- Must prove family relationship (birth certificate, marriage certificate)
- Both parties must be present at DLD or have POA

SUCCESSION PLANNING CHECKLIST FOR UAE PROPERTY OWNERS:
1. Register a UAE will at DIFC Courts ✓
2. Ensure clear co-ownership structure (if applicable) ✓
3. Keep property title deed in a secure location ✓
4. Inform beneficiaries of property ownership ✓
5. Review and update will after major life events ✓
6. Consult a UAE-qualified lawyer for complex structures ✓
